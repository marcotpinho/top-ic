The orienteering problem selects rewarded visits under a route budget~\cite{golden1987orienteering}; its team variant assigns routes to multiple agents with shared reward accounting~\cite{chao1996team}. Surveys document variants with time windows, clusters, and other routing constraints~\cite{gunawan2016survey}. Kinematic orienteering (KOP) couples visit selection with time-optimal trajectories under velocity and acceleration bounds, and studies both a mixed-integer formulation and large-neighborhood search~\cite{meyer2022kop}. We adopt KOP's visit-state perspective but optimize several agents and evaluate their chronological information exchange. Our finite velocity menu and deterministic connector are implementation choices; they do not imply globally optimal continuous trajectories.

Communication-aware team orienteering is represented most directly by TOP-CC, which combines task reward, route expenditure, and communication quality in a multi-objective search~\cite{topcc}. Work on connected robot teams also studies maintaining a communication graph during movement~\cite{yang2010decentralized}. Our model differs in allowing disconnection, charging flight time rather than treating path length as energy, and measuring bounded information staleness instead of an instantaneous distance or signal-quality objective. We do not present a numerical TOP-CC comparison because the current experiments do not implement a matched baseline under our kinematic and temporal assumptions.

Intermittent-connectivity planning has used scheduled encounters and temporal-logic specifications~\cite{kantaros2016intermittent,kantaros2017temporal}. Klaesson et al. address information consistency for exploration with intermittent connectivity~\cite{klaesson2019intermittent}. Other exploration work plans intermittent rendezvous through a scheduling formulation~\cite{silva2023rendezvous}, while a recent adaptive-window method weighs the travel cost of communication against exploration benefit using an orienteering subproblem~\cite{rossano2026mace}. These papers motivate explicit information exchange, but their task and motion models differ from the finite-reward, acceleration-constrained team orienteering problem studied here. Our chronological propagation rule is essential: an aggregated contact graph can be connected even when its edge order cannot carry a source's data to a receiver within the window.
