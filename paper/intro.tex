Teams of mobile robots can visit more spatially distributed targets than a single vehicle, which is useful in environmental monitoring, infrastructure inspection, and disaster assessment~\cite{mansfield2021multirobot}. Team orienteering formalizes the allocation of rewarded visits under one budget per route~\cite{chao1996team}. For aerial robots, however, distance alone is an incomplete cost: acceleration and velocity limits determine whether a route can be flown within its budget~\cite{meyer2022kop}. Communication adds a second operational need because observations gathered by separated agents may have to reach the rest of the team before they become stale.

We study the Team Orienteering Problem with Intermittent Communication and Continuous Kinematics (TOP-ICC). Agents choose task assignments, visit order, visit velocities, and optional reward-free waypoints. Consecutive visits are joined by continuous double-integrator trajectories, and every agent must respect an individual flight-time budget. Agents exchange their newest information whenever they are within communication range. The difficult coupling is temporal: a union of contact edges can be connected although their order does not permit information to propagate within the required recurrence time. Route edits can therefore change reward, flight time, and downstream information age at once.

Prior work separately provides strong foundations for team orienteering, kinematic orienteering, connectivity-aware routing, and intermittent rendezvous~\cite{gunawan2016survey,meyer2022kop,topcc,kantaros2017temporal,silva2023rendezvous}. The closest communication-aware orienteering model optimizes reward, route expenditure, and communication quality~\cite{topcc}, whereas intermittent-connectivity planners emphasize scheduled meetings or mission specifications~\cite{kantaros2016intermittent,kantaros2017temporal}. These approaches do not directly resolve the combination considered here: shared task reward, acceleration-constrained continuous trajectories, temporary disconnection, and a bound on time-respecting all-to-all information age. Our aim is to formulate that combination precisely and provide a reproducible search procedure; the present study does not claim optimality or superiority over unmatched baselines.

Our approach keeps communication feasibility separate from the two reported objectives, team reward and mean completion time. A continuous-time evaluator extracts range contacts from piecewise quadratic trajectories and performs a chronological information sweep. A seeded multi-objective variable-neighborhood search maintains a feasible epsilon archive $\mathcal F$ and a bounded pool $\mathcal P$ of valid stepping stones, then applies ruin-and-recreate, communication repair, and route, assignment, velocity, relay, and free-waypoint neighborhoods.

The paper makes four verifiable contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a continuous-time TOP-ICC formulation with explicit task-credit rules, bounded double-integrator motion, and directed information-age recurrence (Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation});
    \item an exact event-based communication evaluator for the represented trajectories, including simultaneous relay and left-limit age peaks (Section~\ref{sec:method});
    \item a reproducible MOVNS that combines a feasible reward--time archive with a three-coordinate pool for communication-infeasible candidates (Section~\ref{sec:method}); and
    \item saved experiments that expose both feasible behavior and current search limitations rather than presenting an unmatched superiority claim (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
