We formulated team orienteering with acceleration-constrained motion and intermittent, time-respecting information exchange. The implemented search jointly chooses rewarded visits, agent assignments, route states, and optional free rendezvous locations while reporting the three-objective frontier in reward, mean completion time, and communication-bound violation. Continuous contact extraction and chronological propagation avoid the false feasibility that can arise from time-aggregated contact graphs.

The current experiments establish reproducible feasible solutions and one small instance where a free waypoint helps. They also expose the main limitation: a short run reaches few outer iterations and often retains convoy-like feasible routes despite much higher-reward budget-valid candidates. The next work is to improve compound split-and-rejoin proposals and communication repair, examine longer and multi-seed runs, and compare against a matched continuous-connectivity baseline. Collision avoidance, obstacles, and physical energy models are outside the present formulation.
