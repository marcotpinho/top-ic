\subsection{Protocol and Artifacts}
We report completed, saved runs rather than projected performance. Four deterministic synthetic maps represent clustered post-disaster tasks, a curved wildfire front, a branching pipeline corridor, and a uniform agriculture field. All have three agents, axis limits of $3\,$m/s and $1.5\,$m/s$^2$, and a $0.5\,$s handshake. The results below use the archived threefold-flight-budget variants of these maps. Their task positions, rewards, motion limits, and communication settings are unchanged from the source maps; only each agent's flight-time budget is tripled. The maps contain 64, 55, 48, and 180 tasks, respectively. Their geometry is stylized and contains no obstacles.

Each map receives one MOVNS run with seed 42, a 115-second optimizer allowance, a 120-second process timeout, and limits of one million candidate evaluations and one million outer iterations. The first limit reached stops the run. The archive epsilon widths are one reward unit, $0.1\,$s mean time, and $1\,$s positive communication violation. Free waypoints are capped at three per agent. Two maps run concurrently. Full configuration, source hashes, trajectories, archives, logs, and plots are saved in the repository's \texttt{results/step4/scenarios\_search\_guidance\_triple\_budget\_20261012\_4maps} folder. An independent reconstruction checker validated all four saved epsilon fronts.

A separate, deliberately small two-agent relay probe tests whether a free waypoint can improve a feasible route. Its saved best feasible solution collects reward 146 with mean completion time $55.13\,$s, peak staleness $9.11\,$s below $T_c=12\,$s, and a FREE visit at $(67.4,0)$. A saved same-reward feasible candidate without a FREE visit has mean time $57.65\,$s. This is a within-run example of benefit, not a controlled ablation or a general performance estimate.

The budget multiplier is not a monotonicity claim about the heuristic. A larger physical budget enlarges the feasible set, but a fixed-time stochastic search need not return a better incumbent. The archived original-versus-tripled comparison summary illustrates this: post-disaster's best saved feasible reward fell from 311 to 186 when the larger budget changed the search trajectory. We do not infer a causal improvement or deterioration from one seed. Matched multi-seed comparisons, longer search budgets, communication-neighborhood ablations, and a kinematically matched TOP-CC baseline remain future experimental work.

\subsection{Effect of the Recurrence Bound}
Figure~\ref{fig:tc_contact_sweep} gives a spatial view of how the recurrence bound changes the routes and their contact portions. We use the same two-agent instance, communication range $R=2\,$m, seed 103, and 900-second search budget in all three panels. The plotted solution is the maximum-reward member of the final feasible archive, with ties broken by lower mean completion time. The smallest value in this campaign, $T_c=0.05\,$s, is used as a near-zero case. It yields $Q=300$, $\bar c=18.62\,$s, and numerical-zero peak staleness. The intermediate case $T_c=5\,$s yields $Q=390$, $\bar c=17.71\,$s, and $\Delta=4.49\,$s. \subsection{Indirect Communication Through a Relay}
We use a three-agent bridge instance to expose indirect propagation geometrically. The agents start and finish at $(10,0)$ and have separate tasks at $(10,-6)$, $(20,4)$, and $(0,4)$. With $R=14.5\,$m and $T_c=0.05\,$s, the selected solution assigns one task to each agent, collects the full reward $Q=150$, has mean completion time $9.81\,$s, and has numerical-zero peak staleness and $CV=0$. Agents 2 and 3 have no direct contact from $t=3.438\,$s to $t=7.261\,$s, but both remain directly connected to Agent 1 throughout this $3.823\,$s interval. Their snapshots therefore propagate through the component $2\text{--}1\text{--}3$ without a direct $2\text{--}3$ link.

\subsection{Matched TOP-CC Comparison at Constant Connectivity}
We compare the current TOP-ICC implementation with an adapted constant-connectivity baseline, TOP-CC-C0, built from TOP-CC revision \texttt{38c863a}. Both methods receive the same four original-budget maps, task rewards, three-agent team, communication range, seed 42, and nominal 600-second wall-clock budget. Each map pair runs concurrently with one numerical-library thread per process. TOP-ICC uses its acceleration-bounded model and exact $T_c=0$, which requires the instantaneous range graph to remain connected throughout the mission. TOP-CC-C0 retains piecewise-linear motion at constant scalar speed $3\,$m/s, the RSSI objective, and archive size 40. Its distance budget is $3B_m$, where $B_m$ is the original temporal budget, and its \texttt{multi\_vis} variant permits shared visits.

The unmodified TOP-CC initializer did not save a continuously connected solution in a preliminary campaign, even with tripled budgets. We therefore add one deterministic feasible initializer: tasks are inserted greedily by reward per added distance into a common route, and every agent receives that route. The resulting convoy is continuously connected and respects the converted budget. This plays the same feasibility role as TOP-ICC's cooperative initialization, but it is an additional heuristic and must be included when interpreting the baseline. We otherwise retain the original TOP-CC operators and dominance relation. After each run, all saved paths are post-validated by exact continuous range-graph connectivity and distance budgets. The agriculture TOP-CC-C0 alarm was armed 10 seconds early to absorb a non-interruptible Numba batch; its effective search time was $591.57\,$s, so it received slightly less than the nominal budget.

Table~\ref{tab:topcc_comparison} reports the maximum-reward connected solution saved by each method, breaking ties by mean completion time. TOP-CC-C0 saved higher reward on all four maps: $70.3\%$ versus $56.3\%$ of available reward on post-disaster, $100\%$ versus $99.1\%$ on wildfire, $56.1\%$ versus $8.6\%$ on pipeline, and $21.9\%$ versus $2.8\%$ on agriculture. However, on post-disaster, pipeline, and agriculture its sole saved connected solution was the greedy convoy seed; only wildfire retained multiple connected alternatives. Thus these rows primarily compare TOP-ICC against the added convoy construction rather than demonstrate consistent improvement by the original TOP-CC search. TOP-ICC peak staleness was at most $1.43\times10^{-14}\,$s, i.e., numerical zero.

The methods also differ substantially in work completed within the wall-clock budget. TOP-CC-C0 evaluated 233,396, 188,331, 336,574, and 57,651 candidates, whereas TOP-ICC evaluated 25,715, 17,062, 38,947, and 26,717, respectively. These counts are not normalized units of effort because the evaluators and motion models differ. The TOP-ICC batch diagnostic recorded 12, 10, 7, and 23 batch--scalar divergences, respectively; every batch-feasible candidate is re-evaluated by the scalar evaluator before archive insertion, so the table uses scalar-validated incumbents. Together with the added TOP-CC-C0 initializer, single-seed design, slightly shorter agriculture baseline run, and distinct kinematics, this prevents a statistical or purely algorithmic superiority claim; the table establishes only the quality and feasibility of the solutions actually saved under this protocol.
