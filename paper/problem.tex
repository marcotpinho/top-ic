\subsection{Informal description}
TOP-ICC asks a team to collect valuable tasks and reach a common destination before individual flight-time limits expire. Routes may contain reward-free waypoints that create useful encounters. Communication is intermittent: agents exchange all currently held information whenever their continuous trajectories are within range, and received information may be forwarded at later encounters. A solution is communication-feasible when every directed source--receiver information age stays below a prescribed recurrence bound. Among feasible solutions, we seek the trade-off between collected team reward and mean completion time.

\subsection{Sets, parameters, and notation}
Let $\mathcal A=\{1,\ldots,M\}$ index agents, $\mathcal V=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ index tasks, and $\mathcal D=\{x,y\}$ index spatial axes. Task $i$ has position $z_i\in\mathbb R^2$ and reward $r_i\ge0$. All agents share start $z_S$ and destination $z_E$. Agent $m$ has initial velocity $v_m^S$, flight-time budget $B_m$, and componentwise velocity and acceleration limits $\bar v_{md}$ and $\bar a_{md}$. Communication range is $R$ and the recurrence bound is $T_c$.

\subsection{Decision variables}
A solution $S$ assigns each agent an ordered sequence $\pi_m=(\nu_{m0},\ldots,\nu_{m,K_m-1})$. The first occurrence is START at $z_S$, the last is END at $z_E$, and each interior occurrence is either TASK, fixed at some $z_i$, or FREE, whose position is chosen by the search and earns no reward. Every occurrence has a nonzero boundary velocity $v_{mk}$. The route sequence, task assignment, free-waypoint coordinates, and interior velocity magnitudes are decisions. Interior velocity directions are derived from adjacent route positions; START and END use the prescribed boundary velocities. Arrival and departure coincide at every visit, so one velocity state is shared by the incoming and outgoing connectors. Routes contain no explicit waiting decision.

\subsection{Objectives and Pareto concepts}
Let $\mathcal Q_m$ be the task IDs represented by explicit TASK occurrences in $\pi_m$. Repeated or shared visits credit each task once:
\begin{equation}
 Q(S)=\sum_{i\in\cup_{m\in\mathcal A}\mathcal Q_m}r_i,
 \qquad \bar c(S)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m\in\mathcal A}c_m.
 \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
The feasible archive has one scalar function for each reported objective:
\begin{align}
 f_Q^{\mathcal F}(S)&=Q(S) &&\text{(maximize)},\nonumber\\
 f_c^{\mathcal F}(S)&=\bar c(S) &&\text{(minimize)}.
 \label{eq:objectives}
\end{align}
For feasible solutions $S$ and $S'$, $S$ dominates $S'$ in $\mathcal F$ when $f_Q^{\mathcal F}(S)\ge f_Q^{\mathcal F}(S')$ and $f_c^{\mathcal F}(S)\le f_c^{\mathcal F}(S')$, with at least one strict inequality. The Pareto set contains feasible solutions not dominated by another feasible solution. We approximate it with an epsilon-box archive. For implementation and hypervolume calculation only, the maximized reward is sign-reversed to obtain the equivalent minimization vector $(-f_Q^{\mathcal F},f_c^{\mathcal F})$. Search progress is measured by normalized two-dimensional hypervolume with reference point $(1,1)$ after scaling reward by total available reward and time by the largest flight budget; larger hypervolume is better.

\subsection{Motion, budget, and reward constraints}
Consecutive visits are joined by a double-integrator trajectory:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \dot p_m(t) &= v_m(t),\\
  \dot v_m(t) &= a_m(t),\\
  |v_{md}(t)| &\le \bar v_{md},\\
  |a_{md}(t)| &\le \bar a_{md}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:dynamics}
\end{equation}
These constraints enforce continuous position and velocity and bound each velocity and acceleration component. For an interior occurrence, let
\begin{equation}
 d_{mk}=\frac{p_{m,k+1}-p_{m,k-1}}{\|p_{m,k+1}-p_{m,k-1}\|_2}
 \label{eq:geometric_heading}
\end{equation}
be its geometric velocity direction when the denominator is nonzero. Given an optimized speed fraction $\rho_{mk}$, the boundary velocity is
\begin{equation}
 v_{mk}=\rho_{mk}s^{\max}_{mk}d_{mk},\qquad
 s^{\max}_{mk}=\min_{e\in\mathcal D:\,|d_{mke}|>0}\frac{\bar v_{me}}{|d_{mke}|}.
 \label{eq:visit_velocity}
\end{equation}
Thus $\rho_{mk}$ controls timing while the route geometry controls direction. At a sharp turn, the implementation caps $\rho_{mk}$ as a function of the angle between the incident route segments.

For fixed endpoint states, we use the KOP time-optimal connector~\cite{meyer2022kop}. For each axis $d$, it considers the classical and synchronization acceleration patterns
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_{md}=\left\{
 \begin{aligned}
  &(+\bar a_{md},0,-\bar a_{md}),\\
  &(-\bar a_{md},0,+\bar a_{md}),\\
  &(+\bar a_{md},0,+\bar a_{md}),\\
  &(-\bar a_{md},0,-\bar a_{md})
 \end{aligned}
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:kop_patterns}
\end{equation}
For a pattern $b\in\mathcal B_{md}$, durations $t_{1,md},t_{2,md},t_{3,md}\ge0$ must satisfy the endpoint position and velocity, the componentwise bounds, and
\begin{equation}
 t_{1,md}+t_{2,md}+t_{3,md}=D_{mk}.
 \label{eq:axis_synchronization}
\end{equation}
The same $D_{mk}$ is imposed on both axes. We enumerate analytical boundaries of the reachable-duration sets and choose the smallest duration for which at least one pattern is feasible on every axis. This explicitly handles synchronization intervals that may be disconnected; it does not assume $D_{mk}=\max_d D^{\min}_{mkd}$. Combining the axis switching times yields finitely many vector pieces with quadratic position, linear velocity, and acceleration components in $\{-\bar a_{md},0,+\bar a_{md}\}$. Acceleration may jump at switching times, so jerk is not bounded.

If $D_{mk}$ is the resulting duration from occurrence $k$ to $k+1$, then
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{m0}=0,\qquad \alpha_{m,k+1}=\alpha_{mk}+D_{mk},
 \qquad c_m=\alpha_{m,K_m-1}\le B_m.
 \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Thus each final arrival must respect its individual budget. The mission horizon is $T=\max_m c_m$. After completing its route, an agent remains at $z_E$ until $T$ for communication evaluation without increasing $c_m$. Reward is earned only by explicit TASK occurrences; incidental flyovers and FREE occurrences at task coordinates earn nothing.

\subsection{Temporal communication constraint}
At time $t$, agents $m$ and $n$ communicate when
\begin{equation}
 \|p_m(t)-p_n(t)\|_2\le R.
 \label{eq:contact}
\end{equation}
Each agent stores its newest snapshot of every agent, including snapshots received indirectly. Let $h_{nm}(t)$ be the timestamp of the newest snapshot originating at source $m$ that receiver $n$ holds, with $h_{nm}(0)=0$ and $h_{mm}(t)=t$. At a communication event, every connected component $C$ of the instantaneous range graph merges its stored snapshots. For every receiver $n\in C$ and source $m\in\mathcal A$,
\begin{equation}
 h_{nm}(t^+)=\max_{q\in C}h_{qm}(t^-).
 \label{eq:snapshot_exchange}
\end{equation}
Thus an agent $q$ may transmit its stored snapshot of source $m$ even when $q\ne m$; agents need not contact the original source directly. Snapshots are retained after separation and can be forwarded again at later contacts, yielding store--carry--forward propagation. Define
\begin{equation}
 A_{nm}(t)=t-h_{nm}(t),\qquad
 P_{nm}(S)=\sup_{0\le t\le T} A_{nm}(t).
 \label{eq:staleness}
\end{equation}
The supremum includes the left limit immediately before an exchange. Communication feasibility is represented by
\begin{equation}
 CV(S)=\sum_{n\ne m}\max\{0,P_{nm}(S)-T_c\}=0.
 \label{eq:communication_constraint}
\end{equation}
This constraint requires every directed information-age peak to remain within $T_c$. Equivalently, every full mission window of length $T_c$ admits time-respecting propagation between every ordered pair. When $M=1$, we define $CV=0$.

\subsection{Assumptions and complexity}
We assume known task positions and rewards, deterministic dynamics, perfect localization, isotropic range communication, instantaneous and lossless exchange while in range, and initial synchronization. Collisions, obstacles, jerk limits, bandwidth, and physical energy are outside the model. These assumptions isolate the coupling among reward allocation, bounded motion, and temporal information propagation; flight time is therefore a resource proxy rather than an energy claim. Visit velocities are selected from a finite menu and FREE positions from a bounded workspace, which makes the implemented search finite but does not imply global optimality for the underlying continuous problem.

TOP-ICC is NP-hard. With one agent, communication constraint~\eqref{eq:communication_constraint} is vacuous and the model contains kinematic orienteering; fixing the motion model to the routing-cost special case recovers the classical orienteering problem, which is NP-hard~\cite{golden1987orienteering,meyer2022kop}. Additional agents and temporal contacts enlarge rather than simplify the decision space.
