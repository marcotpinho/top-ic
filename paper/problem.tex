\subsection{Informal description}
TOP-ICC asks a team to collect valuable tasks and reach a common destination before individual flight-time limits expire. Routes may contain reward-free waypoints that create useful encounters. Communication is intermittent: agents exchange all currently held information whenever their continuous trajectories are within range, and received information may be forwarded at later encounters. A solution is communication-feasible when every directed source--receiver information age stays below a prescribed recurrence bound. Among feasible solutions, we seek the trade-off between collected team reward and mean completion time.

\subsection{Sets, parameters, and notation}
Let $\mathcal A=\{1,\ldots,M\}$ index agents, $\mathcal V=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ index tasks, and $\mathcal D=\{x,y\}$ index spatial axes. Task $i$ has position $z_i\in\mathbb R^2$ and reward $r_i\ge0$. All agents share start $z_S$ and destination $z_E$. Agent $m$ has initial velocity $v_m^S$, flight-time budget $B_m$, and componentwise velocity and acceleration limits $\bar v_{md}$ and $\bar a_{md}$. Communication range is $R$ and the recurrence bound is $T_c$.

\subsection{Decision variables}
A solution $S$ assigns each agent an ordered sequence $\pi_m=(\nu_{m0},\ldots,\nu_{m,K_m-1})$. The first occurrence is START at $z_S$, the last is END at $z_E$, and each interior occurrence is either TASK, fixed at some $z_i$, or FREE, whose position is chosen by the search and earns no reward. Every occurrence has a nonzero boundary velocity $v_{mk}$. The route sequence, task assignment, free-waypoint coordinates, and boundary velocities are decisions. Arrival and departure coincide at every visit; routes contain no explicit waiting decision.

\subsection{Objectives and Pareto concepts}
Let $\mathcal Q_m$ be the task IDs represented by explicit TASK occurrences in $\pi_m$. Repeated or shared visits credit each task once:
\begin{equation}
 Q(S)=\sum_{i\in\cup_{m\in\mathcal A}\mathcal Q_m}r_i,
 \qquad \bar c(S)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m\in\mathcal A}c_m.
 \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
The reported minimization vector is
\begin{equation}
 f(S)=\bigl(-Q(S),\bar c(S)\bigr).
 \label{eq:objectives}
\end{equation}
For feasible solutions $S$ and $S'$, $S$ dominates $S'$ when $f_j(S)\le f_j(S')$ for both objectives and the inequality is strict for at least one. The Pareto set contains feasible solutions not dominated by another feasible solution. We approximate it with an epsilon-box archive. Search progress is measured by normalized two-dimensional hypervolume with reference point $(1,1)$ after scaling reward by total available reward and time by the largest flight budget; larger hypervolume is better.

\subsection{Motion, budget, and reward constraints}
Consecutive visits are joined by a double-integrator trajectory:
\begin{equation}
 \dot p_m(t)=v_m(t),\quad \dot v_m(t)=a_m(t),\quad
 |v_{md}(t)|\le\bar v_{md},\quad |a_{md}(t)|\le\bar a_{md}.
 \label{eq:dynamics}
\end{equation}
These constraints enforce continuous position and velocity and bound each velocity and acceleration component. The implementation represents a connector by finitely many constant-acceleration pieces and computes a minimum-time connection for fixed endpoint states. If $D_{mk}$ is the duration from occurrence $k$ to $k+1$, then
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{m0}=0,\qquad \alpha_{m,k+1}=\alpha_{mk}+D_{mk},
 \qquad c_m=\alpha_{m,K_m-1}\le B_m.
 \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Thus each final arrival must respect its individual budget. The mission horizon is $T=\max_m c_m$. After completing its route, an agent remains at $z_E$ until $T$ for communication evaluation without increasing $c_m$. Reward is earned only by explicit TASK occurrences; incidental flyovers and FREE occurrences at task coordinates earn nothing.

\subsection{Temporal communication constraint}
At time $t$, agents $m$ and $n$ communicate when
\begin{equation}
 \|p_m(t)-p_n(t)\|_2\le R.
 \label{eq:contact}
\end{equation}
Every connected component of the instantaneous range graph exchanges its newest information. Let $h_{nm}(t)$ be the newest timestamp originating at $m$ that receiver $n$ holds, with $h_{nm}(0)=0$ and $h_{mm}(t)=t$. Information is retained and may be forwarded at later contacts. Define
\begin{equation}
 A_{nm}(t)=t-h_{nm}(t),\qquad
 P_{nm}(S)=\sup_{0\le t\le T} A_{nm}(t).
 \label{eq:staleness}
\end{equation}
The supremum includes the left limit immediately before an exchange. Communication feasibility is represented by
\begin{equation}
 CV(S)=\sum_{n\ne m}\max\{0,P_{nm}(S)-T_c\}=0.
 \label{eq:communication_constraint}
\end{equation}
This constraint requires every directed information-age peak to remain within $T_c$. Equivalently, every full mission window of length $T_c$ admits time-respecting propagation between every ordered pair. When $M=1$, we define $CV=0$.

\subsection{Assumptions and complexity}
We assume known task positions and rewards, deterministic dynamics, perfect localization, isotropic range communication, instantaneous and lossless exchange while in range, and initial synchronization. Collisions, obstacles, jerk limits, bandwidth, and physical energy are outside the model. These assumptions isolate the coupling among reward allocation, bounded motion, and temporal information propagation; flight time is therefore a resource proxy rather than an energy claim. Visit velocities are selected from a finite menu and FREE positions from a bounded workspace, which makes the implemented search finite but does not imply global optimality for the underlying continuous problem.

TOP-ICC is NP-hard. With one agent, communication constraint~\eqref{eq:communication_constraint} is vacuous and the model contains kinematic orienteering; fixing the motion model to the routing-cost special case recovers the classical orienteering problem, which is NP-hard~\cite{golden1987orienteering,meyer2022kop}. Additional agents and temporal contacts enlarge rather than simplify the decision space.
