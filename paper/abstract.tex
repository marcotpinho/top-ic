\begin{abstract}
We study a team orienteering problem in which agents collect rewards under individual flight-time budgets while exchanging information intermittently. Each route consists of explicit task and optional free-waypoint visits connected by acceleration- and velocity-constrained trajectories. Communication occurs whenever trajectories remain within range long enough to complete a handshake; information can then propagate through simultaneous connected components and later time-respecting contacts. We measure the maximum age of any agent's information at another agent and minimize reward loss, mean completion time, and violation of a prescribed staleness bound. We implement a multi-objective variable-neighborhood search with ruin-and-recreate perturbations, route-state dynamic programming, continuous contact evaluation, and an epsilon-dominance archive. Preliminary experiments on four synthetic maps demonstrate feasible solutions and expose a large gap between the best communication-feasible and budget-only candidates under short search limits. A separate two-agent probe demonstrates a useful free waypoint. These results characterize the current solver; they do not establish superiority over existing methods.
\end{abstract}
