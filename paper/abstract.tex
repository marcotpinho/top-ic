\begin{abstract}
We study team orienteering with individual flight-time budgets, acceleration-constrained motion, and intermittent communication. Routes contain explicit task visits and optional reward-free waypoints connected by continuous double-integrator trajectories. Agents exchange their newest information whenever they are within range; simultaneous and later contacts can relay information through the team. Feasibility bounds every directed source--receiver information age by a prescribed recurrence time, while the reported objectives maximize unique team reward and minimize mean completion time. We formulate this continuous-time problem and develop a seeded multi-objective variable-neighborhood search with diverse initialization, ruin-and-recreate, communication repair, a feasible epsilon archive, and a bounded pool of valid stepping stones. An event-based evaluator extracts contacts from piecewise quadratic trajectories and propagates information in chronological order, including simultaneous relay and left-limit age peaks. Saved preliminary experiments demonstrate feasible solutions and expose current search limitations; they characterize the solver without claiming optimality or superiority over unmatched baselines.
\end{abstract}
